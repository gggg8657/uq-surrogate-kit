## The three walls, and why they are walls

**1. `≥100×` and `a calibrated interval` are the same knob, pulled opposite ways.**
The interval and the OOD score are both computed from the ensemble spread, and
the spread does not exist with one member.

| M | Darcy speedup | coverage | corr(spread, error) |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **108.5×** ✅ | — *no spread* | — *no spread* |
| 2 | 58.5× | 91.7% | +0.951 |
| 5 | 23.1× | 90.2% | +0.960 |

Every configuration that clears 100× has no uncertainty; every configuration
with uncertainty is below it.

**2. Against a good solver at matched accuracy the speedup is 2.2×, and for
four of five families the surrogate is 15–220× *slower*.**
Poisson, Helmholtz, diffusion and advection–diffusion have exact spectral
propagators — two FFTs, 0.2 ms for a batch of 64. A neural surrogate is simply
the wrong tool there, and those rows are kept in the table rather than curated
out. Darcy is the only iterative solve, and its 26.8× headline is against a PCG
converged to 1e-10. Loosen the tolerance to 1e-1 and the solver still reaches
rel-L2 8.5e-5 — **500× more accurate than the surrogate's 4.3%** — in 48 ms
against the surrogate's 22 ms. The sweep never made the solver as inaccurate as
the surrogate, so even 2.2× is an upper bound.

**3. An operator shift with matched inputs is undetectable by anything
unsupervised — as an identity, not as a tuning gap.**

| shard | rel-L2 | `spread` | `mahalanobis` | `residual` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `biharmonic` | **41.25** | 0.486 | 0.497 | 0.495 |
| `frac_s3` | **1702.5** | 0.499 | 0.492 | 0.502 |

The surrogate is wrong by a factor of 40 to 1,700 and all three detectors sit at
chance. They must: each is a function of the input and the *configured*
operator, and these shards change neither by construction. A model configured as
Poisson, handed `f`, returns the Poisson solution — its spread is
in-distribution because the input is, and its Poisson residual is small and
*correct*. It is a good answer to a question nobody asked. The same detectors
reach 1.000 on shifts of comparable severity whose inputs do move
(`darcy_c3`, rel-L2 0.77), which rules out "the ensemble is too small".

## What did not work, and what that rules out

- **Weighted conformal does not rescue coverage under shift**, except in a
  narrow window: it lands in band at Δα ≤ 0.2 (`darcy_dam0p1` 90.8%,
  `poisson_dam0p2` 91.8%) and beyond that the density-ratio probe saturates at
  AUC 1.00, the supports stop overlapping, and the exact weighted quantile
  correctly returns **+∞ for 512 of 512 test points**. Rules out: "the shift
  problem is a reweighting problem". It is a support problem.
- **The dominant coverage failure under shift is *over*-coverage**, not under:
  24 of 32 shards sit at 99–100%. The σ-scaled score grows with the shift, so
  the band widens faster than the error does. Rules out: "locally adaptive
  scores are conservative in the right direction". They are uninformative in it.
- **A physics residual is not a general error detector.** Its noise floor scales
  as `N^(operator order)`: 5.1e-5 for Poisson at 64², **53** for a sixth-order
  operator. Above fourth order it reports round-off. Rules out: "check the PDE
  residual" as a universal trust signal.
- **An ensemble jittered in the wrong variable measures nothing.** On the toy
  reactor, members differing by rate-constant jitter give error-detection AUROC
  **0.550**; the same members differing by *step size* give **0.999**. Rules
  out: "any ensemble gives usable uncertainty".

## What does work

- **In-distribution calibration is solid** on all four scores (88.6–89.8%
  pooled) and the spread–error correlation is +0.86 to +0.96 on four of five
  families (Darcy +0.46).
- **One labelled probe closes the hole.** Run k samples through the reference
  solver and compare their errors to the calibration distribution with a
  conformal p-value: **k = 1** gives 95% power at a *measured* 5% false-alarm
  rate on every shard the unsupervised detectors could not see. And it correctly
  declines to alarm (k > 64) on all ten resolution shards, where `mahalanobis`
  fires at AUROC 1.000 despite the error not moving at all.
- **The kit is genuinely simulator-agnostic**, and CI proves it: the whole
  pipeline runs on a three-ODE batch reactor with a multi-fidelity surrogate, on
  CPU, in `tests/test_api.py`.

## Two decisions for a human

**Decision 1 — what to do with A4.** Options, in my order of preference:

- **(a) Re-scope the KPI to what the measurements support and ship it.**
  "90±2% in distribution and under covariate shift up to a probe AUC of 1.00;
  OOD AUROC ≥0.9 on 43/49 shards with the four exceptions characterised as an
  identity; speedup reported as iso-accuracy." This is an honest, unusual and
  defensible artifact, and the negative results are the interesting part.
- **(b) Keep the KPI and change the problem** so the speedup clause can be met:
  3D, or a stiff-chemistry solver, or fine meshes — anywhere the solver is
  expensive at the accuracy anyone actually needs. A 64² periodic box is not
  such a place. This is a new project, not a fix to this one.
- **(c) Keep the KPI and change the uncertainty** to a single-forward-pass
  variance head, which removes the M× cost. Recovers at most 108× at M=1's
  accuracy; still 2.2× iso-accuracy, so it fixes one wall of three.

**Decision 2 — GPU 2.** It is held at **2.88 TFLOP/s against GPU 3's 44.07**
(15.3×) by **91 leaked CUDA contexts, 48 GB, whose PIDs do not exist**. Not
mine, not killable from this namespace, and the boundary rules forbid touching
another loop's GPUs. Options: (a) leave it and run the track at half its lease —
what I did, and it cost nothing here because a member trains in 5 minutes;
(b) someone with the right privileges resets the device. This affects every
track that has GPU 2 in its lease.

## The one thing I got wrong, and caught

The first `RESULTS.md` reported **40.8×** as the Darcy headline. It was measured
across the H100's clock ramp — the surrogate had boosted and the solver had not.
Repeating one configuration eight times showed a **±36%** swing; adding a
6-second ramp before any measurement brought it to **±1%** and the number to
23.5×. The error was in the direction that flattered the result, and it was
larger than several differences turn 2 had discussed as findings. The weekend's
seed-count lesson is not about seeds — it is about any comparison whose noise
floor is unmeasured, and a timing harness is an instrument like any other.
