# A4 — UQ Surrogate Kit — status: **RUNNING** (was `UNREACHABLE`; the structural wall is gone and clause 2 now has a met reading)

Every number below is regenerated by `scripts/report.py` from `runs/*.json` and
checked against those JSONs by `scripts/check_prose_numbers.py` in CI. Long form:
[`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md), [`critique_log.md`](critique_log.md).

**The one-line version.** Friday's verdict was `UNREACHABLE` on the argument
that ≥100× and a calibrated interval were mutually exclusive. They were — but
only because the interval was built out of an ensemble. Putting the interval
inside **one** network dissolved that, which is the real result here: the
structural blocker was our own design decision, and it is dead.

**Clause 2 is met at batch 1 — 24/24 fields, worst 117.0× — and the route there
is the whole lesson.** Three rounds went into removing subsidies from our own
*reference solver* (a per-iteration synchronization, face coefficients rebuilt
every iteration on a fixed field, and a convergence stride tuned on one field
and imposed on the rest), each verified to leave the solver's answer
**bit-identical**. That took the speedup 602× → 328× → 228× → 21/24 fields,
worst 94.0×, i.e. to a fail. We published the break-even ("a reference reaching
63.73 ms on the hardest field ends the clause") before the run that ended it;
the field that broke it solves in 63.77 ms.

Then we finally audited the *surrogate* and found the same class of defect on
our own side, untouched through three rounds of scrutiny: `SpectralConv2d`
contracted with `einsum("bixy,ioxy->boxy", …)`, which must permute a 13.1 MB
weight tensor on **every forward pass** although the weights are fixed at
inference. A batch-1 kernel census took four minutes and said the arithmetic was
5% of the forward (8 matmuls + 8 FFTs = 65.6 µs) against 258.7 µs of `copy_`.
Caching the permuted weights is worth **1.246×**, bit-identically, and it is
what took the worst field from 94.0× to **117.0×**.

**The lesson, which cost three turns:** auditing only the side whose improvement
hurts your claim feels like rigour and is a blind spot with a flattering shape.
It guarantees you find every reason your number is too high and none of the
reasons it is too low.

## What changed, and why it was a design error rather than a wall

**The interval no longer costs M forward passes.** Friday's blocker was an
identity: the interval and the OOD score both came from ensemble spread, spread
needs M>1, and only M=1 cleared 100× (`108.5×` at M=1, `58.5×` at M=2, `23.1×`
at M=5). So "fast" and "has an interval" could never be the same row. A
heteroscedastic σ head plus the same split-conformal machinery puts the interval
in one forward pass: **0.9026** coverage, 8/8 seeds in band. The coverage row and
the speedup row are now one model and one run.

**Read the control before believing that.** A *constant* σ also lands 8/8 in
band, because split conformal rescales any σ to ~90% marginal coverage. So
coverage-in-band at M=1 is not evidence the head learned anything. The head's
only real effect is width: the `het` head is **13.1% sharper** than the constant
control. That is the honest size of this result and it is much smaller than the
clause it satisfies.

**The 100× clause was decided by a subsidy in our own benchmark.** `solve_darcy`
defaulted to `check_every=1` — a device-to-host sync on every one of up to 2000
PCG iterations — and `bench_speedup.py` timed the reference solver that way while
timing the surrogate with autograd left on. Removing both:

- **228.2×** at batch 1 under CUDA-graph replay (gated bit-exact against eager,
  then re-gated against a *mutated* input so a stale buffer cannot pass);
- **72.3×** for the eager arm against the fair denominator, versus 107.6× against
  the subsidized one. **The subsidy alone was the difference between passing and
  failing this clause**, on the protocol every earlier number here used.

**Then we attacked our own denominator twice more, and clause 2 crossed back
under the line.** (a) The reference rebuilt four face-coefficient arrays on
every PCG iteration although the coefficient field is fixed for the whole solve;
hoisting them out is bit-identical and cut the solve 1.28×. (b) It
preconditioned with the *continuous* Laplacian symbol while applying a 5-point
FD stencil — a ~2.5× mismatch at Nyquist — so the matching discrete symbol cut
the iteration count 839 → 775. (c) Worst of all, **our own sweep had selected
one convergence stride on one field and imposed it on all 24**, which makes the
easiest fields slower and had *inflated* our worst case from 107.9× to 129.3×.
We caught that because a better reference should never make our own ratio go up.
Giving the solver its best admissible stride per field: **21/24**, worst
**94.0×** — a fail.

**Then we audited the surrogate for the first time, and it had the same class of
defect.** A batch-1 kernel census (`runs/profile_surrogate.json`) attributed the
0.6437 ms closure: the spectral convolutions are 49% of it, and inside them the
arithmetic is 5% — 8 matmuls and 8 FFTs total 65.6 µs against **258.7 µs of
`aten::copy_`**. `einsum("bixy,ioxy->boxy", …)` lowers to a batched matmul over
the mode grid, so it permutes a 13.1 MB weight tensor into `(x, y, in, out)`
order on every forward pass although the weights never change at inference.
Caching the permuted weights and contracting with `bmm` is **1.246×**.

*Same model, not a faster approximation of it.* `max|Δ| = 0` — bit-identical,
the same bar the solver's fixes had to clear — on the mean **and** both interval
bounds, on **64/64** data shards at N=64, 128 and 256
(`runs/packed_equivalence.json`), and `rel_l2` is unchanged to every stored
digit. So every coverage, sharpness and OOD number already published stands
without a re-run.

**Clause 2 is MET at batch 1: 24/24 fields, worst 117.0×, median 187.4×**, at
rel-L2 0.0506 against an FP64 PCG reference converged to 1e-10 (achieved
5.0e-11), under CUDA-graph replay, one forward pass emitting mean and interval
together. The check that makes it credible is paired: the three fields that had
failed (17, 18, 2) had denominators that moved by **under 1%** between the two
runs while their ratios rose **23–25%**, matching the surrogate's own 1.246×.
Two other fields show 1.7× denominator swings, which is inside the 1.71 max/min
this repo already measured on identical solver work — those two rows carry no
information and are not quoted.

**Three conditions come with that ✅ and none of them is a footnote.** Batch 64
is **41.1×** — the solver batches *better* than we do (1.41× wall clock for 64×
the arithmetic, against our 8.5×). Without CUDA-graph capture the same 24 fields
are **2/24**. And the reference is still not equally optimized: it is a Python
PCG loop launching individual kernels, and fused stencil kernels, cached
grid-dependent preconditioner data and graph-captured PCG chunks are admissible
and unmeasured. At a 0.512 ms surrogate a reference reaching **51.2 ms** on a
field ends the clause on it, and the fastest field solves in **60.3 ms** — a 15%
margin, against three previous solver rounds that bought 1.28×, 1.08× and a
selection fix. The defensible sentence is *"24/24 measured fields against this
specified PCG implementation"*, not an advantage over an equivalently optimized
reference.

**OOD is not a detector-family limit after all.** The old 0.486–0.503 on
operator shift (`0.486`/`0.497`/`0.495` on `biharmonic`, `0.499`/`0.492`/`0.502`
on `frac_s3`) was an identity: every detector was a function of (input,
configured operator) and those shards change neither. Scoring the residual of
the *requested* operator escapes it — `param_oor` 4/4 and `unseen_operator` 3/3,
where all three old detectors sat at chance.

## What did not work, and what that rules out

- **Amortizing the solver's convergence sync (H9).** Predicted the batch-1
  solver would drop below 120 ms; it went 214.85 → 189.81 ms, **1.13×**. The
  solver is launch-bound but not *sync*-bound, so that diagnosis is withdrawn.
- **Believing a speedup that had only ever been measured on one input.** Three
  rounds of reference-solver fixes took clause 2 from apparently met by 6× to
  not met. Every round was verified bit-identical. This rules out "the surrogate
  is orders of magnitude faster than the solver" as a claim about this problem
  at this accuracy: it is a factor of **117–315** depending on the field, and
  the spread across fields (2.70×) is comparable to the margin to the target.
- **Auditing only the side whose improvement hurts you.** Three consecutive
  turns went into the reference solver and none into the surrogate, and the
  surrogate turned out to be carrying a 1.246× memcpy of constants in a file
  that had been read several times. This is the methodological finding of the
  weekend and it generalizes: one-sided scrutiny is not conservatism, it is a
  bias with a flattering shape.
- **CUDA graphs as a general speedup.** At batch 64 the graph arm is *slower*
  than eager (4.981 vs 4.902 ms). It is a batch-1 latency technique, nothing
  more, and the table shows it losing where it loses.
- **Coverage under covariate shift, without labels.** Still fails, but **half
  of the stated reason was ours and is now withdrawn.** We reported "30/32
  shards return an infinite quantile, so their 100% coverage is abstention" and
  attributed it to a distribution-free impossibility. An infinite weighted
  quantile is in fact impossible whenever `clip² ≤ n_cal·α/(1−α)` — **10.67**
  at our calibration size — and we shipped `clip = 20.0`. Below the bound,
  abstention goes **89.9% → 0.0%** on all 8 seeds and does not depend on shift
  strength at all. What the infinities were hiding is **under**-coverage:
  median coverage **1.000 → 0.116**, cells under 0.88 **0 → 184/256**. The
  honest score is **1.94/32** shards in band (clip selected on held-out shards;
  the tuned 2.75 is selected on the shards it is scored on). It beats the
  `group` calibrator at exact sign-flip **p = 0.0078** and is still nowhere
  near the clause. What survives of the original claim is the *second* half,
  now with a mechanism: on the graded `dam` ladder coverage decays monotonically
  with shift strength on both calibrators and both reach zero at the same
  shift, which is p(y|x) changing rather than only p(x). The constructive
  complement is unchanged: **k = 1** label to notice the shift, nine to
  re-certify the interval at α=0.1.
- **The `navier_stokes` 366× row.** Withdrawn on sight: `trained: False`. An
  untrained network timed against a real 1000-step RK4 solver, accuracy
  `[not measured]`. It is the most seductive number in the repo and it is worth
  nothing.

## Two decisions for a human

**1. Is a batch-1 latency win the deliverable, or a batched throughput win?**
This decides whether clause 2 counts as met. Both readings are in the table and
they now disagree, which is why it is a decision and not a measurement.
- *Option A — ship the latency reading.* **24/24** fields at batch 1 under
  CUDA-graph replay, worst **117.0×**, median **187.4×**. Defensible:
  single-request latency is the plant question, and the surrogate answers in
  0.512 ms with a calibrated interval attached.
- *Option B — hold the batched reading.* **41.1×** at batch 64, clause not met.
  Defensible: at batch 64 both sides are dispatch-efficient, so it is the only
  reading where the comparison is like-for-like on hardware utilisation — and
  the solver batches *better* than the surrogate, which Option A hides.
- *Recommendation:* **A, published with its three conditions inline rather than
  in a footnote** — batch 64 at 41.1×, 2/24 without graph capture, and a 15%
  margin on the easiest field against a reference that has named unmeasured
  optimizations left. Those three rows are already in the KPI table in
  `RESULTS.md` and should travel with the claim wherever it goes. Option B is
  not wrong, but "≥100×" for a plant surrogate is a latency promise in every
  context where anyone would ask for it.

**1b. If Option A is taken, is the remaining risk worth buying down?** The
clause would survive a further 15% improvement in the reference and not much
more. Buying margin is cheap and the routes are named and ranked by an
adversarial review: fusing the block's elementwise work around GroupNorm
(estimated 0.02–0.06 ms of 0.512), pre-caching the bf16 autocast copies of the
fixed 1×1 convolution weights, and packing the spectral bands into reusable
buffers. Doing the reverse — optimizing the *reference* further — is the same
work and would move the number against us, which is the reason to do it. A
decision is needed on which side gets the next turn; the honest default is the
reference, because that is the side a reader will attack.

**1c. Should clause 1 under shift become a *conditional* coverage
specification?** H13 established that the recoverable regime is bounded by the
surrogate's own competence: coverage decays monotonically with shift strength on
both calibrators and both die at the same shift, because past that point p(y|x)
has changed and no reweighting of the inputs can reach it. Meanwhile the OOD
detector already identifies exactly that boundary — **33/33** shards at AUROC
≥0.9 conditional on the shift degrading the model.
- *Option A — keep the unconditional specification.* Clause 1 under shift is
  **not met** at ~2/32 shards and will stay not met. Defensible: an
  unconditional 90±2% is what the KPI says, and a certificate with an escape
  clause is worth less than one without.
- *Option B — certify conditionally.* Emit an interval where the detector says
  the model is in competence, and **abstain deliberately** elsewhere, with the
  abstention rate as a reported quantity rather than an artefact of a clip
  constant. Defensible: it is what a plant would actually want, and it is
  honest in a way the current 100%-coverage-by-infinite-interval cells are not.
- *Recommendation:* **B, but it is a specification change and we are not taking
  it unilaterally.** It converts a failed clause into a met one by changing what
  is being promised, which is precisely the move that needs a human to sign it.
  Note the shape of the trade before signing: Option B's abstention rate would
  be a *measured* number on each shard, so the deliverable becomes "90±2%
  coverage on the x% of inputs we certify", and x is what the buyer is really
  purchasing.

**2. Does clause 3 mean "detect every shift" or "detect every shift that
matters"?** Strict, all 49 shards: **47/49**. Conditional on the shift actually
degrading the surrogate: **33/33**, minimum 0.9964. Both misses are `dam0p1`
at degradation 1.06×, where the model is no worse than in distribution — firing
there is a false alarm, not a detection.
- *Option A —* accept the conditional reading; the clause is met, and the
  criterion is the model's own measured error, not a threshold fitted after
  seeing which shards failed.
- *Option B —* hold the strict reading; the clause misses by two shards that
  nobody would want an alarm on.
- *Recommendation:* A, with the full degradation-vs-AUROC table published so a
  reader can place the cut anywhere. Every AUROC below 0.9 occurs at degradation
  ≤1.06×, so the ordering does the work and no threshold is load-bearing.

**Next hypothesis, written down before it runs (H13 in `critique_log.md`).**
Clause 1's shifted-coverage failure is partly ours. `WeightedConformal` returns
an infinite quantile — abstention, not coverage — exactly when a test point's
importance weight exceeds `W·α/(1-α)`, and with ratios clipped to `[1/clip,
clip]` the worst case makes that `clip² > n_cal·α/(1-α)`. At `n_cal = 1024`,
`α = 0.1` that threshold is **10.67**, and the shipped `clip` is **20.0**. So
abstention on whole test shards is reachable by construction at 20 and
impossible at 10 — which is why the ∞ counts sit at exactly 512 (the whole
shard) on shards whose calibration ESS is a healthy 807/1024. The bound is
pinned in `tests/test_conformal.py`, not asserted. Sweeping `clip` gives either
in-band shifted coverage on shards that currently abstain (clause 1 met on a
reading always available and mis-set by us) or out-of-band coverage (a real
statement about the estimator). Both are worth having.

## The things I got wrong, and caught

- **Our own 40.8× headline** was an H100 clock-ramp artefact (±36% run-to-run,
  now ±1%). Corrected to 23.5× before it was published.
- **The denominator had no stable value and nobody had looked.** Repeating
  identical work 12× reads 221.8–379.9 ms at batch 1 (max/min 1.71) — and across
  *distinct* fields it spans 3.91×, a much bigger effect than the timing noise.
  Every speedup row this repo ever published was a single draw from that.
- **"The dominant failure is over-coverage"** was true of the `split` calibrator
  and false of `group`, the one the in-distribution headline uses, under which
  the dominant failure is *under*-coverage, 33/42, at a median of 70.1%. We had
  reported the harmless failure mode and omitted the harmful one.
- **The staleness gate rejected one of our own rows** — correctly strict, wrongly
  implemented: the input was restored by inverting the arithmetic instead of from
  a saved copy, and floating-point round-trip error grew with tensor size. Fixed
  by an exact restore, not by loosening the tolerance.
- **`codex` found four real defects in the 296× claim**, including that the
  bit-exactness gate could not distinguish a live graph from a cached buffer, and
  that the batch-1 denominator was one repeated sample. Both are fixed above;
  the second one *strengthened* the result.
